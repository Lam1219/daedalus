Proposal · Daedalus · Updated 2026-11-13 · Status: reviewed by the owner on 2026-11-13; direction A accepted

Narrow Daedalus to one job: getting unstuck, on the watch people already wear

This revamp starts from the Nov 11 business case and keeps its core: freezing first, the cue on demand, automatic cues only once proven quiet. Six things change it.

  • 1The first release is cut to the freeze loop: start the beat, set your own pace, and see your week with your doses and last night's sleep beside the times you got stuck. You can share it with your physio and neurologist. Breathing stays because it is built. The Fine motor and Calm vibration modes move behind a setting, and dyskinesia waits. Doses and sleep are your decision of 2026-11-13.
  • 2The beat can also play as sound in earbuds, because the only head-to-head study found a vibrating metronome worked worse than a sound one, and no study has tested the wrist alone.
  • 3Before more building, a three-week home test with the prototype checks the plan's weakest point. Most freezes last under 10 seconds, so the question is whether someone who is frozen can start the beat before the freeze is over.
  • 4The "wellness" route looks weak in both countries. Plan R1 as a Class I device in Canada and ask Health Canada to classify it, instead of relying on a wellness opinion.
  • 5The reachable market is smaller than assumed. The only figures found say about 1 in 5 older adults wears a smartwatch. They are from 2019–20 and not specific to Parkinson's. That would put about 9,000 Canadians who freeze within reach without new hardware. The interviews will measure the real share.
  • 6Platform facts change the build. On Apple Watch the cue must open the app to start, the current workout-session approach is an App Review risk, and all-day recording can only be scored afterwards. The data program needs an independent ethics board either way.

4 · Value proposition and value curve

One press, your own pace, on the wrist or in your ears

Who

People with Parkinson's who freeze and already wear a smartwatch.

Why

Their feet stick at doorways, turns and when they start walking. They feel embarrassed and avoid shops.

What before

Their own tricks, which fade; a laser cane that stays at home; nothing at all outdoors.

How

One press on the watch starts a beat at their own walking pace, as a wrist pulse or a sound in their earbuds. It stops by itself once they are walking.

What after

They get going again quietly, in public too. They and their care team can see when they get stuck, next to their doses and how they slept.

Alternatives

Own tricks, laser cane, metronome app, Parky (not in Canada), CUE1+ (paused).

Value curve

0 = none, 4 = strong. Scores are my judgement from the sources in sections 1 and 2. Daedalus's line is a target for R1, not a measurement, so it flatters Daedalus until the field test measures it.

5 · Recommendation

Three directions; I recommend A

Recommended

A · The freeze loop, narrowed

For people who freeze and own a watch, through their physio. Daedalus does one job well: get unstuck.

The first version does
  • Start the beat from the watch face, a control, the Action button or the app.
  • Play it as a wrist pulse or sound in earbuds, at the person's pace, stopping when they walk.
  • Set the pace with a one-minute walk, with the physio.
  • Show the week: presses by day and hour, shareable with the physio.
  • Let the person set the time a dose was actually taken, with "now" as the default, so a dose logged late still lands at the right time.
  • Put doses (from the existing "Take" button) and last night's sleep (from Apple Health or Health Connect) on the same timeline as the presses, shareable with the neurologist. Never suggest a dose.
  • Keep breathing, which is already built.
It deliberately leaves out
  • Automatic cues (the guard stays silent), detected symptoms in the dose report (R2), dyskinesia, posture. Fine motor and Calm stay in the app, behind a setting (D3).

Trade-off: less to show a clinic or investor, and more riding on one feature.

B · A physio's home-practice tool

For neuro physiotherapists: they prescribe walking practice with a beat, the watch delivers sessions, and they see adherence.

  • Fits better than A under an FDA policy that leaves alone apps helping people manage their own condition, such as home exercise.
  • Doesn't depend on pressing a button mid-freeze.
  • Clinics pay, but they score lowest on pain and willingness to pay.

Trade-off: it helps practice, not the freeze in the doorway, which is the person's real problem.

C · Keep the broad companion

Today's app: four cue modes, tremor check, breathing, medication, then the dose report and dyskinesia.

  • The most features, most of them built.
  • Competes head-on with Parky and StrivePD using the same Apple algorithm.
  • The Fine motor and Calm buzzes have no evidence behind them.

Trade-off: no specific, provable reason for anyone to choose it.

Why A

Freezing is where the pain is (segment 1 scores highest). We found nothing on the watch for freezing in Canada, though Samsung's and Google's own news pages were not searched. A is mostly built, so the test that decides it is cheap. Its outcome is uncertain: if H1 fails, the press trigger changes (section 9). B's physio set-up is kept inside A as the way people start. C spreads the product thin where Parky already sits.

7 · Key screens

The first version, screen by screen

Static, high-fidelity screens in the apps' Sage palette and Manrope type. All names and numbers are sample data. The watch is always shown dark, as watch faces are.

What to look at on each screen

Screen 1: could someone who is shaking or frozen hit it? Screen 2: is "stops when you're walking" enough reassurance? Screen 3: is the physio-led set-up how you want people to start? Screen 4: are presses and time-to-walking the right things to show? Screen 5: would a neurologist read "time since last dose" and the sleep comparison the way you intend? Screen 6: is the consent wording calm and plain? Screen 7: is "Just now" or "Earlier" the right first choice, and should a dose logged hours late be flagged in the report?

Weakest consequential assumption: H1

If people who are frozen cannot start the beat by pressing, or the beat only arrives once a short freeze has ended on its own, a press-started cue fails at the moment it matters. The recommendation would then move to cues that start before a freeze (on standing up, before a doorway) and would bring automatic detection forward, with its regulatory cost.
